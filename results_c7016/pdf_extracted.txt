Experiments with C0 = 70 and L0 = 16
Natural Empirical and Constructed Distributions
OSST project experimental artifact
22 October 2026
Abstract
We execute the real-mean (70, 16) support-size construction on 756 constructed, 236 natural
empirical, and 120 empirically shaped synthetic conﬁgurations. The primary method is evaluated
4,208,000 times, including 1,672,000 actual polynomial-branch trials. No promised binary error or
support-bound failure is observed. However, the same-budget count ablation also has no binary errors,
and a 99%-guaranteed count baseline uses 2.44–8.39 times fewer expected samples on the synthetic
grid.
Polynomial corrections improve 554,000 lower bounds.
We preserve negative comparisons,
ﬁnite-ﬁle failures and dependence stress.

Protocol and statistical scope
We implement the construction in Qisheng Wang’s Optimal Support Size Testing (supplied manuscript
2610.18723v1) with the project’s certiﬁed constants. The algorithm uses L = 1 + ln n, real Poisson
mean m = 70n/(ϵL), and the unchanged polynomial construction when L ≥16.
Its ﬁrst integer
polynomial input is n = 3, 269, 018. We retain all scales, degrees, corrections and decision thresholds;
the largest tested degree is 162. The supplied theorem gives individual failure below 0.282612 for the
binary promise problem and for support-bound coverage. ϵ is the distribution separation parameter,
not the failure probability.
Synthetic sizes range from 1 to 1012 and include three consecutive integers at the branch boundary.
Four epsilons are 0.001, 0.01, 0.1 and 0.25. Uniform, concentrated-head, sparse-tail, power, lognormal,
Gaussian-block, geometric-block, boundary and gap distributions are included. Natural data add ϵ =
0.05. Each primary synthetic/natural cell has four independent blocks of 1000 trials; lifted-shape cells
have four blocks of 500. Exact rational tail masses determine promise labels. Gap inputs have no
binary error label. Code/data hashes were frozen before primary simulation, with no error-dependent
stopping. Diﬀerent budgets use independent streams; same-budget ablations share ﬁngerprints. Method
evaluations are not all independent samples.

Samples, lower bounds and conﬁdence
Single-run samples are about 14.64 times fewer than for (1025, 1024), but the uniform single-run conﬁ-
dence guarantees diﬀer. At the same theoretical conﬁdence target, a certiﬁed count baseline still uses
2.44–16.30 times fewer samples. Even the 99% count baseline uses 2.44–8.39 times fewer samples. This
practical grid has 70/(1 + ln n) ≥2.44, so the count statistic already receives a substantial budget. The
polynomial changes improve lower bounds, not binary rejection counts, on this grid.
On the rare-tail null with n = 1012, ϵ = 0.1 and tail mass 0.1ϵ, the mean observed fraction is
about 0.2169 while the new lower bound is about 0.5786n. Here the eﬀective support is only one; this
illustrates extra information, not recovery from a failed eﬀective-support guarantee.
Using only (70, 16), 27 independent repetitions, majority voting and median aggregation give per-
task failure at most 0.008175637833.
The expected total coeﬃcient is 1890, about 1.844 times the
older coeﬃcient 1025. A union bound for either output failing is 0.016351275666, so we do not claim
simultaneous 99% correctness for both outputs. Fifty conﬁgurations each use 1000 aggregated trials.
The earlier (70, 20) results are not mixed into this experiment.

Natural data and dependence
Ten ﬁnite empirical populations cover mites, birds at two sites, spiders under two treatments, moths,
land snails, BCI trees and ﬁsh from two year/gear strata. Sources, pinned versions, hashes and licenses
are retained in data_sources.json. No diﬀerent years/sites/treatments are pooled. All original natural
populations use the count branch. Splitting each observed category into equally probable synthetic
clones creates separate lifted-shape tests around 107 and 109 support; these are explicitly synthetic, not
new species observations. There are 116 polynomial lifted-shape conﬁgurations.
Only 42 of 236 natural conﬁgurations have enough original records to reach the mean budget. Fixed-
ﬁle tests retain every Poisson overﬂow as Inconclusive, never truncate or resample until success. Suc-
cessful without-replacement subsamples are descriptive and do not establish unconditional iid risk for
an unknown ecosystem. Group holdouts and whole-group compound-Poisson resampling are separate
stress tests. Artiﬁcially duplicated observations produce failure rates up to 100%, despite preserving
marginal labels and expected record count.

Numerical validation and reproducibility
Arb certiﬁes real-mean parameters and full polynomial weights. Batched dot products use outward
enclosures and conservative IEEE-754 roundoﬀbounds; unresolved event thresholds are reﬁned with
Arb. Every binary decision and coverage event is certiﬁed on its supplied counts. Some exact integer
lower outputs remain enclosed in intervals; their means are reported as intervals, not guessed integers.
The public single-ﬁngerprint interface increases precision until it certiﬁes the exact integer output or
raises an error.
There are 8896 full-Arb primary checks, 476 further saved-proﬁle checks at 1024 bits, and 18 explicit
4

full-ﬁngerprint API runs (12 polynomial). All 125 regression tests pass. The analytic proof’s 428 exact
obligations and 2491 independent arithmetic checks are recomputed with byte-identical results. The
ﬁnal audit includes 113,951 checks. This is not an end-to-end proof-assistant formalization.
Grouped multinomial ﬁngerprints have the same law as independent Poisson category counts in real
arithmetic. Probability evaluation and pseudorandom generation remain binary64 approximations. The
high-count bucket retains category counts without fabricating total observations M. No compressed
simulation timing is represented as physical sample-acquisition time.
