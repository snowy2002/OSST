Independent analytic review: small-n upper-tail reduction at C0=70,L0=1

Conclusion: the homogenization theorem and the proposed heavy-category /
zero-singleton reduction are valid. This conclusion concerns the analytic
reduction. Completion of the finite numerical certificates and their exact
index coverage must still be verified separately. No simulation is used in
this review.

1. Homogenization theorem

For 0<=y<1 write d=1-y, b(lambda)=lambda exp(-lambda),
t(lambda)=1-d exp(-lambda), and
h(lambda)=1-(d+lambda)exp(-lambda). The one-coordinate weighted singleton
polynomial is h+bz. For lambda>0, h,b,t are positive, and x=log(t) is
strictly increasing. The following derivatives in homogenization.txt check:

  d(log b)/dx = (1-lambda)(exp(lambda)-d)/(d lambda),
  d/dlambda[d(log b)/dx]
    = [d-exp(lambda)(lambda^2-lambda+1)]/(d lambda^2) <= 0.

Indeed exp(lambda)(lambda^2-lambda+1) starts at 1 and has derivative
exp(lambda)*lambda*(lambda+1)>=0. Thus log b is concave in x.

For log h, the first x derivative is t(lambda-y)/(d h). Its lambda
derivative has positive denominator d exp(2lambda) h^2 and numerator

  N = exp(2lambda)
      -exp(lambda)[lambda^2-lambda+1+d(lambda+1)]+d.

The derivative of N in d is 1-(lambda+1)exp(lambda)<=0. Therefore N is
at least its value at d=1, namely
exp(lambda)[2 cosh(lambda)-lambda^2-2]>=0. The last inequality follows
from the absolutely convergent positive even-power series. Hence log h
is convex in x, including the part where h itself decreases in lambda.

Replacing two x coordinates by their mean keeps t1*t2 unchanged,
increases b1*b2, and decreases h1*h2. The resulting unnormalized
singleton measures have the same total mass and larger tails at both
nonzero cutoffs 1 and 2. This is exactly the order needed for every
nondecreasing function of the singleton count. Conditioning on all
other coordinates preserves the comparison, because their weights are
nonnegative and the conditional test function remains nondecreasing.

The max/min averaging iteration converges: if E=sum_i(x_i-xbar)^2,
one step decreases E by (xmax-xmin)^2/2, and
(xmax-xmin)^2>=E/r. Thus E contracts by at least 1-1/(2r), for r>=2.
This proves convergence to the diagonal, without requiring an attained
global maximizer. The cases r=1 and r=0 are immediate; r=0 should be
stated explicitly when the theorem is used for S=1.

Boundary handling in the supplied proof is correct. If y=0 and a mean
is zero, the weighted expectation is zero. Other zero means and
infinite-mean limits follow from continuity of the finite zero/one/other
probability representation. When y=1, the singleton probabilities are
independent Bernoulli parameters <=1/e, simultaneously maximized by
lambda_i=1. The convention 0^0=1 is consistent.

The computational restriction lambda>=1 also checks. With other
coordinates fixed, the objective is
A+exp(-lambda)[(y-1)A+lambda(B-A)], B>=A>=0. Its derivative is
exp(-lambda)[(1-y)A+(1-lambda)(B-A)]>=0 for 0<=lambda<=1.
Thus the diagonal supremum can be restricted to lambda>=1. The change
of means need not preserve their sum, because this is an upper bound
over an enlarged set of admissible mean vectors.

2. Heavy-category removal

For a finite support S<=n choose h to maximize p_h before observing
counts. Then p_h>=1/S and, with the original real Poisson mean,

  lambda_h = 70 n p_h/(epsilon L)
            >=70 n/(epsilon S L)>70 n/(S L)>=70/L.

Since (1+lambda)exp(-lambda) is decreasing on lambda>=0,
Pr(N_h<=1) is at most (1+lambda_min)exp(-lambda_min), with either
lambda_min=70 n/(S L) or the coarser common value 70/L. The code's
70/L bound is therefore valid uniformly in S and epsilon.

On N_h>=2, the full zero/singleton counts equal those of the remaining
r=S-1 coordinates. Those coordinates retain independent Poisson laws.
Formally, bound the common event on the remaining coordinates without
conditioning and add Pr(N_h<=1). Independence also justifies conditioning,
but dropping the probability of N_h>=2 already gives the desired bound.

3. Envelope, strict integer threshold, and Markov inequality

Write f_j(1)=1+u1, f_j(ell)=1+(-1)^(ell+1)u_ell for 1<=ell<=k,
f_j(0)=0, and f_j(ell)=1 for ell>k. Set
q=max(0,max_{odd ell>=3,ell<=k} u_ell). The empty maximum gives q=0.
On N_h>=2,

  Z_j <= (1+q)S-(1+q)F0+(u1-q)F1.

Consequently Z_j>(1+delta)S+delta*n implies

  F0 < T_j(F1),
  T_j(s)=[(u1-q)s+(q-delta)S-delta*n]/(1+q).

For a group of scales with u1>=q, every T_j is nondecreasing in s.
Taking their maximum preserves monotonicity. For integer F0 the strict
condition is exactly F0<=ceil(max_j T_j(s))-1. The minus one is essential
when the threshold itself is an integer; using floor alone would fail
at integer thresholds. The Arb unique-ceil routine correctly rejects
uncertified boundary values instead of guessing.

Thus z_s=ceil(max_j T_j(s))-1 is nondecreasing. For 0<y<1, define
phi_y(s)=0 if z_s<0 and phi_y(s)=y^(-z_s) otherwise. This phi is
nonnegative and nondecreasing, and pointwise

  1{F0<=z_F1} <= y^F0 phi_y(F1).

The homogenization theorem now applies. For r=S-1 its diagonal bound is
sum_{s=0}^r binom(r,s)b(lambda)^s h(lambda)^(r-s)phi_y(s), maximized
over lambda>=1. The thresholds still use the original S and n; only the
number of remaining random coordinates changes to r. The implementation
passes S-1 and z[0:S], which is the correct range.

The actual code uses the stronger good-scale condition q<=delta<=u1.
This implies u1>=q and additionally z_0<0. Thus the r=0 case has an empty
common event, as handled by its zero_event path. All other scales go to
the separate MGF group. Adding their individual bounds, the common
event bound, and the heavy-category error is a valid union bound; no
independence between the scales is asserted or needed.

4. Uniform one-variable certificate in small_exact.certify_common

For lambda>=1, b(lambda)=lambda exp(-lambda) is nonincreasing and
h(lambda)=1-(1-y+lambda)exp(-lambda) is increasing, with h>0.
On [a,b] the sum of nonnegative monomials is therefore bounded by

  h(b)^r P(b(a)/h(b)),
  P(v)=sum_s binom(r,s)phi_y(s)v^s.

On [R,infinity), b(lambda)<=R exp(-R) and h(lambda)<=1, giving the
valid tail bound P(R exp(-R)). The finite interval subdivision and this
tail cover all lambda>=1. Numerical y optimization only proposes a
rational y; a successful outward-interval certificate for that rational
y suffices even if it is not optimal.

5. Required coverage safeguards for the independent certificate replay

The analytic reduction is sound, but small_exact.certify currently uses
zip(bad_scales, saved_mgf_proposals) and iterates saved common proposals.
Without extra checks, a shortened input artifact could omit a scale or
a support size. The final independent verifier should require:

* The exact final n index set, including n=74 if the next range begins at 75.
* For each n, exactly one common certificate for every S=1,...,n.
* Disjoint good/bad groups whose union equals every prescribed scale.
* Exact j,k,delta correspondence for every saved low-scale MGF proposal.
* Recomputed z_s values, z_s nondecreasing, and complete s=0,...,S-1.
* Certified rational 0<y<1, positive targets/t/eta, and all per-cell bounds.
* The full union bound, plus the separately proved lower-deviation budget,
  is strictly below 1/3. The small-n upper bound alone does not prove the
  joint theorem.

Negative tests deleting one n, one S, or one low scale should be rejected.
These are verification-completeness requirements, not a mathematical
counterexample to the proposed proof. This review did not modify the
small-n implementation or claim that all outstanding numerical certificates
had already been completed.
