Finite n upper-tail certificate for C=70, L0=1

We consider a distribution with finite support size 1<=S<=n; for S>n
the support-estimator upper bound is automatic after clipping at n.
All counts are independent Poisson(lambda_i), where sum lambda_i=m and
m=70n/(epsilon L), L=1+log n, 0<epsilon<1.
Scale j has delta_j and alternating corrections
 f_j(0)=0, f_j(ell)-1=(-1)^{ell+1}u_{j,ell} for 1<=ell<=k_j,
 f_j(ell)-1=0 for ell>k_j, with u>=0.
Its false-positive event is
 Z_j>(1+delta_j)S+delta_j n.
This is exactly the event that its corrected statistic exceeds S.

1. Splitting the scales
The program partitions scales into separately controlled scales and a
common-event group. The partition can vary with n; its chosen value is
recorded, then checked independently of the numerical optimizer.
For a common-event scale define
 q_j=max(0,max_{odd ell>=3}u_{j,ell}).
The program checks u_{j,1}>=q_j. Every count ell>=2 has correction<=q_j,
including even counts (nonpositive corrections) and counts above k_j.
Consequently
 Z_j <= S-F0 +u_{j,1}F1 +q_j(S-F0-F1).
Its false-positive event is therefore contained in
 F0 < [(u_{j,1}-q_j)F1+(q_j-delta_j)S-delta_j n]/(1+q_j).
Let z_s be ceil of the maximum of the right sides over the group, minus
one. Outward Arb arithmetic certifies the ceil. Thus the union event is
contained in F0<=z_{F1}. The sequence z_s is nondecreasing, because every
slope (u_{j,1}-q_j)/(1+q_j) is nonnegative. No condition q_j<=delta_j is
needed for this reduction. If z_0>=0 the bound is generally unhelpful,
and the numerical proposal stage tries a different scale partition.

2. A necessary use of the total-mass constraint
The unconstrained supremum over Poisson means is too loose at very small
n: it discards sum lambda_i=m. Choose a fixed largest-probability category
of p, before sampling, and call it h. Since S<=n,
 lambda_h>=m/S>70n/(SL)>=70/L=:a_n.
The function (1+a)e^{-a} decreases for a>=0. Hence
 Pr(N_h<=1) <= (1+a_n)e^{-a_n}=:eta_h(n).
For the two certificate rows n=16 and n=27 we use the slightly stronger
event N_h>=K with K=4; all other rows use K=2. For an even K>=2 put
 q_{h,j}=max(0,max_{odd ell>=K+1}u_{j,ell}).
On N_h>=K the anchor's correction is at most q_{h,j}, while F0 and F1
count only the other r=S-1 categories. The sharper common threshold is
 [(u_{j,1}-q_j)s+(q_j-delta_j)S-delta_j n-q_j+q_{h,j}]/(1+q_j).
For K=2 it is the previous threshold. We do not change S to S-1 in this
formula. The program verifies the alternating signs of all corrections.
It uses the sharper anchor mean 70n/(SL) and charges the full bad-event
probability
 eta_h(n,S,K)=exp[-70n/(SL)] sum_{ell=0}^{K-1}[70n/(SL)]^ell/ell!.
Poisson stochastic monotonicity justifies using this lower mean.
Consequently the common-event probability is at most eta_h(n) plus the
probability of F0_remaining<=z_{F1_remaining}. Counts of the other fixed
categories remain independent; no conditioning or data-dependent choice
of h is used in this probability bound.

3. Exponential majorant and homogenization
Choose an exactly represented rational 0<y<1 and define
 phi_y(s)=0 if z_s<0, and phi_y(s)=y^{-z_s} otherwise.
This is nonnegative and nondecreasing. Pointwise,
 1{F0<=z_{F1}} <= y^{F0} phi_y(F1).
The analytic theorem proved in homogenization.txt bounds its expectation
by the supremum for r=S-1 equal Poisson means. In notation from that proof,
 b(lambda)=lambda e^{-lambda},
 h(lambda)=1-(1-y+lambda)e^{-lambda},
 the required one-dimensional function is
 Q_{n,S,y}(lambda)=sum_{s=0}^r binom(r,s)
                  b(lambda)^s h(lambda)^{r-s} phi_y(s).
Its supremum may be restricted to lambda>=1, as proved there.
The r=0 case is evaluated directly.

4. Rigorous certification of the entire one-dimensional domain
For lambda>=1, b decreases and h increases, since
 b'=(1-lambda)e^{-lambda}<=0,
 h'=(lambda-y)e^{-lambda}>0.
On any interval [a,b] with a>=1, positive coefficients therefore give
 Q(lambda)<=sum_s binom(r,s)b(a)^s h(b)^{r-s}phi_y(s).
All terms are evaluated by 512-bit outward-rounded Arb arithmetic.
The program starts from [1,R], R=24, and bisects until each whole closed
interval has a strict upper bound below the proposed rational target.
It records every leaf and does not infer positivity from sampled points.
For lambda>=R, h<=1 and b(lambda)<=R e^{-R}, so the whole remaining
half-line is bounded by sum_s binom(r,s)(R e^{-R})^s phi_y(s).
This is also checked strictly below the same target.
The exact rational y and target are proposals only; unsuccessful interval
checks cause the verifier to stop. The optimizer is not part of the proof.

5. The other scales
For each separately controlled scale use its exact centered MGF,
 G_j(t,lambda)=1+e^{-lambda}sum_{ell=0}^{k_j}
                  (exp(t*y_ell)-1)lambda^ell/ell!,
 where y_0=-1 and y_ell=f_j(ell)-1. In particular the negative zero-count
term is retained. The verifier in mgf.py certifies G_j<=1+eta_j for all
lambda>=0 by an exponential Taylor lower polynomial, positive Bernstein
coefficients on a finite interval, and nonnegative shifted coefficients
on the remaining half-line. It then bounds the scale's error by
 exp[S log(1+eta_j)-t delta_j(n+S)].
This exponent is affine in S. The proposal stage can optimize its worst
S endpoint, but the final assembly evaluates the exact exponent for each
specific S, rather than summing unrelated maxima over S.

6. Assembly
For each (n,S), sum eta_h(n,S,K), its own common-event rational target,
and all other-scale MGF bounds evaluated at that S. The maximum over
S=1,...,n is a rigorous upper-tail bound. This controls all scales jointly
without pretending that their
statistics are independent. It also controls false rejection for the
binary tester when S<=n, because exceeding n implies exceeding S.

The small_certificates.json result records the checks and bound for each
n. Its all_passed flag requires each assembled bound to be less than
1/3-10^{-20}; the independently certified lower-tail error is much smaller
than this reserved margin. This proof covers all epsilon in (0,1) and
all finite support distributions with S<=n, not only simulated instances.
