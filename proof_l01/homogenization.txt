Homogenization of zero/singleton Poisson occupancy statistics

Theorem. Let X_i be independent Poisson(lambda_i), F_0=# {i:X_i=0},
F_1=# {i:X_i=1}, and let phi:{0,...,r}->[0,infinity) be nondecreasing.
For every y in [0,1],
 sup_{lambda_1,...,lambda_r>=0} E[y^{F_0} phi(F_1)]
 = sup_{lambda>=0} E_{lambda_1=...=lambda_r=lambda}[y^{F_0} phi(F_1)].
The supremum may be a limit at infinity. Take 0^0=1.

Proof. First assume 0<d:=1-y<=1 and all relevant parameters are finite
and positive. Put
 a=e^{-lambda}, b=lambda e^{-lambda},
 t=1-d e^{-lambda}, h=t-b=1-(d+lambda)e^{-lambda}.
The unnormalized generating polynomial for a single variable, after
weighting zero outcomes by y, is h+bz. Its total mass is t. Both h and
b are positive for lambda>0, and x=log t increases strictly with lambda.

We need two curvature facts in this x coordinate.

(i) log b is concave as a function of x. Indeed,
 d(log b)/dx = (1-lambda)(e^lambda-d)/(d lambda),
 and its derivative with respect to lambda equals
 [d-e^lambda(lambda^2-lambda+1)]/(d lambda^2) <= 0.
Here e^lambda(lambda^2-lambda+1)>=1, since its value at zero is 1 and
its derivative is e^lambda lambda(lambda+1)>=0. Since dx/dlambda>0,
the claimed concavity follows.

(ii) log h is convex as a function of x. Its first derivative is
 R(lambda)=t(lambda-y)/(d h).
Direct differentiation gives
 R'(lambda) = N(lambda,d)/(d e^{2lambda} h^2),
 N(lambda,d)=e^{2lambda}
             -e^lambda[lambda^2-lambda+1+d(lambda+1)]+d.
N decreases with d because its d derivative is 1-(lambda+1)e^lambda<=0.
Consequently, for 0<d<=1,
 N(lambda,d)>=N(lambda,1)
 = e^lambda[2cosh(lambda)-lambda^2-2] >= 0.
The last inequality follows termwise from the absolutely convergent
cosh series: 2cosh(lambda)-lambda^2-2
=2 sum_{k>=2}lambda^{2k}/(2k)! >=0. This proves convexity.

Given two parameters, replace their x coordinates by their arithmetic
mean. The corresponding common lambda_* is uniquely defined by
 t_*^2=t_1 t_2. The curvature facts imply
 b_*^2>=b_1 b_2 and h_*^2<=h_1 h_2.
For the two-variable unnormalized singleton distribution, total mass,
mass above or equal to one, and mass above or equal to two are respectively
 t_1t_2, t_1t_2-h_1h_2, b_1b_2.
After replacement they become t_*^2, t_*^2-h_*^2, b_*^2. Thus total mass
is unchanged and both positive tails increase. Every nondecreasing phi
is a nonnegative linear combination of a constant and upper-tail
indicators, so its expectation does not decrease. Conditioning on all
remaining variables preserves this assertion, since their weights are
nonnegative and k->phi(k+F_1_other) is nondecreasing.

Apply this operation repeatedly to the largest and smallest x coordinate.
The mean of all r coordinates stays fixed and their squared deviations
decrease by half the squared distance of the replaced pair. The squared
range is at least the variance divided by r, so the variance tends to zero.
All coordinates converge to their common arithmetic mean. Continuity
then shows that the original expectation is at most the expectation at
this single common lambda. The reverse inequality follows by inclusion
of diagonal parameter tuples in the original supremum.

Boundary cases follow by continuity. If y=0 and some lambda_i=0 then
t_i=0, and the whole weighted expectation is zero, so the inequality is
immediate. Zero singleton weights and lambda=infinity are limits.
Finally, if y=1, weighting disappears and F_1 is a sum of independent
Bernoulli variables with success probabilities lambda_i e^{-lambda_i}.
Their probabilities are all maximized by lambda_i=1, and a nondecreasing
phi is maximized by increasing each probability. This proves the claim
also for y=1. QED.

Useful corollary for computation. For 0<y<1, the diagonal expectation is
 sum_{s=0}^r binom(r,s) b(lambda)^s h(lambda)^{r-s} phi(s).
Its supremum can be restricted to lambda>=1: with other variables fixed,
the objective equals A+e^{-lambda}[(y-1)A+lambda(B-A)], where B>=A>=0.
Its derivative is e^{-lambda}[(1-y)A+(1-lambda)(B-A)]>=0 on [0,1].
Increasing all coordinates up to 1 therefore cannot decrease it.

The proof is analytic and independent of any numerical optimization.
