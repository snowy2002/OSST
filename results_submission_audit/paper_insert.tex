\section{Finite-constant implementation and empirical evaluation}
\label{sec:finite-experiments}
We evaluate the original statistic with the refined constants
$(C_0,L_0)=(70,14)$ and compare alternative branch thresholds.
The sampling rule remains $M\sim\operatorname{Poi}(m)$ with
$m=C_0n/(\varepsilon\log(en))$; all prescribed polynomial scales and
degrees are retained. The finite-constant analysis gives single-run
failure below $0.0009$ for $(70,14)$ and below $0.000057$ for $(70,16)$.
These are analytic guarantees, not estimates obtained from the simulations.
The expected sample coefficient is reduced by $93.17\%$ relative to the
previous certified value 1025. This comparison concerns sampling means,
not measured running time or dominance over other testers.

\paragraph{Design and scope.}
The primary constructed grid uses $n\in\mathcal N$, where
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N=\{&1,2,10,10^3,442413,442414,1202604,1202605,\\
             &3269017,3269018,24154952,24154953,10^9,10^{12}\},
\end{aligned}
\]
and $\varepsilon\in\{0.001,0.1\}$, including both sides of each tested branch
transition. We additionally use ten published abundance datasets as finite
empirical distributions, category-split variants of those distributions,
targeted head-and-rare-tail nulls, and diffuse alternative controls.
Category splitting creates synthetic distributions; it does not add
natural observations. The natural experiments use
$\varepsilon\in\{0.01,0.1\}$ and thresholds chosen from the empirical
support and effective-support boundaries. These choices use population
truth to design evaluation instances, not to compute the tested statistic.

Each method--configuration cell has 2,000 Monte Carlo trials in four
independently keyed blocks of 500. A trial is one algorithm run, not a
majority vote over the blocks. The four $C_0=70$ threshold variants share
their sampled fingerprints; different constants use separate random
streams. In total there are 4,424 method cells, 8,848,000 method--trial
evaluations, and 5,116,000 independently generated simulation paths.
The methods are $C_*=144(\log2)^2,L_0=14$, $C_0=70$ with
$L_0\in\{14,15,16,18\}$, and $C_0=1025,L_0=1024$, with smaller
constants included only in designated stress tests.

\paragraph{Risk and statistical precision.}
The recommended pair has no observed binary error in 1,792,000 promised
trials and no support-bound failure in 1,994,000 coverage trials.
For each cell we also reconstruct the event that either required output
fails; on gap instances only coverage is required. One-sided
Clopper--Pearson bounds with Bonferroni correction over all 4,424 method
cells give a simultaneous 95\% upper bound of approximately $0.56791\%$
for every zero-error cell. This finite-grid uncertainty is larger than the
analytic risk guarantees. We therefore do not interpret the absence of
errors as empirical confirmation of their precise numerical values or as
a distribution-free empirical proof. Heterogeneous cells are not pooled
to assert a worst-case risk bound.

\paragraph{What the polynomial contributes on this grid.}
At the same sampling mean, returning the clipped observed count also has
no observed binary or coverage failures on the recommended method's grid.
Indeed, for all $n\le10^{12}$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$, a separate discovery
coupling and interval-certified Poisson bound give pure-count error below
$1.863\times10^{-12}$ at this mean. Thus the current experiments cannot
demonstrate a binary-power advantage of the polynomial at the certified
budget. Their positive polynomial result concerns the magnitude of the
reported support lower bound: 442,000 of the recommended method's trials
have a numerically certified increase over the clipped observed count.
In the 622 paired primary configurations, lowering $L_0$ from 16 to 14
gives a strictly positive certified sample-mean increase in 50 configurations.
A post-hoc one-sided Hoeffding analysis of paired gains normalized by $n$,
with simultaneous 95\% coverage over all 622 configurations, establishes
a positive expected gain in 14 configurations. This conservative result
does not imply an absence of expected gain in the other configurations.

\paragraph{Stress tests and natural-data limitations.}
The targeted head-and-rare-tail nulls expose false rejection for smaller
constants. For example, a tested $C_0=60,L_0=14$ configuration has 127
errors in 2,000 trials ($6.35\%$), whereas its $C_0=70$ comparator has
none on the same population. The targeted population grids differ across
constants, so their maxima are stress diagnostics rather than a common-grid
risk curve. The finite tests at 69 also show no failures; this is compatible
with the necessary-constant theorem, which uses an asymptotic sequence
with $\varepsilon$ depending on $n$.

The ten empirical datasets contain 50,994 original records and between
4 and 240 observed categories each. All unmodified empirical instances
use the counting branch. Their iid resampling experiments assess finite
empirical populations and cannot establish the number of unseen species
or independence of field observations. Only 13 of the 76 empirical
configurations have expected budgets no larger than the available record
count, and even those do not guarantee completion of a Poisson draw from
a fixed file. Sampling with replacement from a file supplies more
simulation trials, not more independent field observations.

\paragraph{Numerics and reproducibility.}
Equal-probability category groups allow multinomial fingerprint simulation
without enumerating every category. This implements the ideal Poisson
fingerprint law in real arithmetic; the sampling probabilities and random
generator are standard binary64 approximations. Scores and relevant
decision thresholds are enclosed using outward floating bounds with Arb
refinement. In 37,071 recommended-method trials the saved integer lower
output is an interval rather than a single resolved integer; the binary
and coverage events are nevertheless resolved. Lower-bound comparisons
use conservative interval endpoints, not their midpoints.

The artifact contains exact rational populations and truth labels, keyed
seeds, raw fingerprints, event arrays, source/data hashes, and code for
reconstructing all reported counts. A documented precision amendment for
the $C_0=6$ stress test increased arithmetic precision after a numerical
failure; completed raw outcomes were retained. Grouped simulation timings
do not measure physical sample collection, and cached batch scoring is not
an end-to-end runtime comparison. The current experiments support a
finite-constant and lower-bound-strength study; they do not establish
universal empirical superiority over counting or prior support testers.
