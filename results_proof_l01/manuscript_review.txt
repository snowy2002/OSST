Independent review of C70_L1_proof.tex
Date: 2026-11-04
Scope: analytic proof structure, theorem scope, range coverage, and correspondence
to the supplied certificates. This review does not itself replace the independent
deterministic replay of the full certificate package.

Overall assessment
The principal analytic chain is correct. I found no counterexample or missing
parameter range in the stated C0=70,L0=1 sufficient-constant result. The new
homogenization argument, common F0/F1 envelope, deterministic anchor, positive
one-dimensional interval enclosure, full-line MGF certificate, and discovery
coupling fit together correctly. The old (70,14) theorem explicitly covers the
same real Poisson mean, countable support, and simultaneous binary/interval
statement, so transferring it for L>=14 is legitimate.

The draft needs the local clarifications below before being called fully
self-contained. None requires changing the estimator or the calculated bounds.

1. Clarify the anchor event in the common-event probability statement
Location: around line 377, immediately before the diagonal formula.
The sentence currently says that homogenization bounds the probability of the
union of the selected scales by the diagonal supremum. For K=4, the sharpened
threshold was derived only on N_h>=K, so the literal unconditional statement
omits the anchor failure. The next paragraph adds this failure and the numerical
calculation is correct, but the intermediate sentence should explicitly say:
  P((union of selected upper-scale events) intersect {N_h>=K})
  <= sup_lambda Q_{n,S,y}(lambda).
Then add P(N_h<K) and the remaining-scale bounds. The required fixed choice of
h, before sampling, and the absence of conditioning on the other counts are
already correctly explained.

2. Make the q definition agree with the K=2 equality
Location: lines 340 and 364.
The first line permits any q_j >= max(0,max_odd>=3 u), whereas the definition of
q_{h,j} uses the exact maximum. Under this more general wording, K=2 gives
q_{h,j}<=q_j, not necessarily equality, so “For K=2 this equals T_j” need not be
literally true. The implementation takes the exact maximum enclosed by Arb.
Use q_j := max(0,max_odd>=3 u) in the analytic exposition, allowing an outward
interval enclosure for computation. Alternatively retain the general envelope
and say the K=2 threshold is at least as sharp as the previous one, rather than
claiming equality. Defining the maximum over {0} union the indicated finite
set also settles empty-degree cases without a convention for max(empty set).

3. Attach or precisely identify the inherited large-range proof
Location: around line 417.
The transfer itself is valid. I checked results_parameter_search/proof.tex:
its theorem uses m=Cn/(epsilon L) without a ceiling and states simultaneous
binary/support correctness for finite or countable p. Its assembly is
max(U+R_f,R), explicitly handling S<=n, S>n, and the gap. Thus there is no
implicit sum of two separate 1/3 bounds in the inherited result.
However, the current new TEX ends without appendix inclusion, while its prose
says the earlier proof is “reproduced in the attached appendices.” To make that
true, the final deliverable must actually include or bundle and link:
  results_parameter_search/proof.tex (sufficiency theorem and analytic bounds),
  artifacts/joint_constants_submission/submission_proof.tex (the expectation,
  coefficient/range, and three-case lower-deviation ingredients it invokes).
The precise earlier theorem and these dependency files should be named in the
new manuscript or bibliography. The numerical large_range.json check alone
cannot replace their analytic reductions. A combined PDF appendix or an
explicitly described self-contained proof package both resolve this issue.

4. Make countable-support well-definedness explicit
Location: after defining Z_j around line 58.
Add one sentence: sum_i N_i is Poisson(m), hence finite almost surely; because
f_j(0)=0, D and every Z_j are finite almost surely even for countable support.
The MGF product used in the new upper proof is only needed when S<=n, so is a
finite product. When S>n, the upper bound is automatic after clipping. The
discovery-coupling lower argument already covers countable support. The old
large-range proof also explicitly justifies its countable sums by integrability
and L2 convergence. No new finite-alphabet assumption is needed.

5. Preserve the explicit margin when adding lower-tail error
The displayed macro U_small<0.331 alone cannot be added to a positive lower
error while retaining the same 0.331 result. The draft correctly cites the
aggregate rational-margin check and also gives the sharper actual upper bound
<0.330679396. For reader clarity, explicitly state
  0.330679396 + 1.657e-27 < 0.331,
and compare the other ranges' maxima similarly or invoke the global maximum.
The aggregate verifier's actual Arb computation does perform this margin check.
This is a presentation clarification, not an uncovered numerical obligation.

6. Describe safe threshold rounding as a full-expression enclosure
The strict event F0<T produces z=ceil(T)-1, as used in the code. This handles
an exactly integral threshold correctly. When describing interval computation,
prefer “enclose the entire threshold expression and certify its ceiling” over
an unqualified suggestion to substitute coefficient upper endpoints: the
expression has both additions and subtractions and rational dependence on q.
The current Arb implementation already encloses the whole expression and
rejects an unresolved ceiling. No implementation change is needed.

Checks passed in this review
- The Chebyshev coefficient identity and d_{k,r+1}/d_{k,r} recurrence agree.
- The geometry proof covers all L>=1 and retains every scale and degree ceiling.
- Removing b<=1 is justified by the original Appendix A proof, which never uses
  this restriction in its three x regions or coefficient argument.
- The discovery thinning produces iid Bernoulli(epsilon) marks independent of
  arrival times, and D<=r implies at most r marked arrivals. Taking
  r=min(e_epsilon,n)-1 controls interval underestimation; r=n controls the
  binary alternative. For the first case r=0, the clipped lower bound 1 is
  already automatic.
- The MGF polynomial implication is correct: eta*T_d-P>=0 and T_d<=exp(lambda)
  imply G<=1+eta. The half-line Bernstein coefficients for d=k have the stated
  factor (k-ell)!/k!.
- Continuous L slabs include all possible active j and ceil(k) branches.
  N=floor(exp(a-1))<=n gives exponent <=-4n/N<=-4. At most 10 scales gives
  10exp(-4)<0.183157.
- The homogenization curvature derivatives, their signs, two-coordinate tail
  comparison, convergence under extreme-pair averaging, and y=0/1 limits are
  valid. Defining 0^0=1 explicitly would remove a minor boundary convention.
- The q envelope is valid for all counts >=2; the K=4 anchor replaces only its
  own q contribution by q_h. The original S remains in every threshold while
  the diagonal polynomial has r=S-1 variables, exactly as required.
- Selected scale events need not be independent; the proof uses a common
  event and a union bound, never multiplies their probabilities.
- For integer S<n, ceil(tilde S)>S iff tilde S>S; clipping cannot introduce an
  upper failure. For S>=n the upper bound is automatic. On the alternative,
  D>n gives both binary No and output n. In the gap only the interval is
  required, with the earlier r argument supplying its lower bound.
- Integer ranges 1..73, 74, 75..403 and continuous range from L=7 cover all
  n>=1 because L(403)<7<L(404). The old range L>=14 overlaps the continuous
  range, so there are no endpoint gaps.
- The manuscript correctly separates uniform proof, empirical confidence
  bounds, the iid empirical-resampling model, and ecological sampling claims.
  It does not claim optimality of Cstar or a practical sampling advantage.
